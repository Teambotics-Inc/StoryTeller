# Research log: The Great Fire of London (1666)

## Assumptions (not asked of the user)
- Subject: the event (2-6 Sept 1666) plus its context (1665 plague, timber city, firefighting practice) and aftermath (rebuilding, insurance, memory/blame). Angle: how a small bakery fire became a catastrophe, who was blamed, and what the ruins taught London.
- Audience: curious general readers. Neutral, non-brand dark "embers" palette (deliberate; an event has no brand).
- Time range ~1665 to the 1680s, with a note on 1830 (removal of the Monument's anti-Catholic wording).
- Credit/disclaimer fixed by the brief: anonymous independent study compiled from cited public sources.
- All sources read 2026-10-01 (accessed). Raw page text was fetched with curl and tag-stripped; quotes were copied from that raw text, except the London Gazette (see gaz85).

## Sources
Tier 1 = primary; 2 = reputable secondary; 3 = tertiary/crowd.

### pepys-02 .. pepys-06: Diary of Samuel Pepys, 2-6 Sept 1666
- https://www.pepysdiary.com/diary/1666/09/02/ (and /03/, /04/, /05/, /06/) · pepysdiary.com (Phil Gyford; Wheatley-edition text) · 2026-10-01 · tier 1 (transcription of a primary text; spelling as on that site; editorial footnotes are NOT Pepys and were not used)
- 2 Sep: Jane woke him "about three in the morning"; "being unused to such fires as followed, I thought it far enough off" (copied). Told King/Duke of York "unless his Majesty did command houses to be pulled down nothing could stop the fire" (copied). Lord Mayor: "Lord! what can I do? I am spent: people will not obey me." (copied). Pitch and tar, oil, wines, brandy in Thames Street.
- 3 Sep: "the streets and the highways are crowded with people running and riding" (copied). Duke of York rode with his guard "to keep all quiet".
- 4 Sep: "Now begins the practice of blowing up of houses in Tower-streete" (copied); W. Hewer reports "Paul's is burned, and all Cheapside".
- 5 Sep: fire reached Barking church and was stopped there; blowing up of houses and workmen from the King's yards helped. "the saddest sight of desolation that I ever saw" (copied). "discourse now begun, that there is plot in it, and that the French had done it" (copied).
- 6 Sep: a fire at Bishopsgate put out; suspicion of a "plot"; "Cloathworkers’ Hall on fire these three days and nights in one body of flame" (copied).

### gaz85: The London Gazette, issue 85, 3-10 Sept 1666 (dated "White-hall, Sept. 8")
- https://www.thegazette.co.uk/London/issue/85/page/1 (scan via /data.pdf) · The Gazette (TSO) · 2026-10-01 · tier 1
- The page is a scanned image with no text layer. I rendered the scan and read it by eye. Quotes are therefore transcribed by me, long-s read as "s", original spelling kept; a human should re-check them against the scan.
- Fire began "at one of the clock in the Morning" in Pudding-lane near New Fish-street; "a violent Easterly wind fomented it"; care "not taken for the timely preventing the further diffusion of it by pulling down houses"; "Divers Strangers, Dutch and French, were, during the fire, apprehended"; concludes "an effect of an unhappy chance" (and the "heavy hand of God"); stopped at Temple Church, Pie-corner, Aldersgate, Cripplegate etc. by Wednesday; flared again at the Temple on Thursday evening; orders about the Tower powder magazines.

### evelyn: The Diary of John Evelyn (ed. Bray), Vol. II, Project Gutenberg
- https://www.gutenberg.org/files/42081/42081-h/42081-h.htm · Project Gutenberg · 2026-10-01 · tier 1 (19th-century edited text of a primary source)
- 3 Sep: "London was, but is no more!"; people "hardly stirred to quench it" (copied); "above 10,000 houses all in one flame" (Evelyn's own estimate).
- 4 Sep: "the stones of Paul's flew like grenados, the melting lead running down the streets in a stream" (copied).
- 5 Sep: blames "tenacious and avaricious men, aldermen" for blocking early house demolition (Evelyn's claim).

## Disagreements recorded in the story (not resolved)
- Deaths: six (Monument, RMG) / nine (TNA) / "fewer than 10" (London Museum); real toll unknowable.
- Churches: 87 (most) / 90 (Gazette editorial); proportion destroyed: 80% (BL), 85% (RMG, of the City), "four-fifths" (Science Museum); 85% of the population homeless (Monument).
- Duration: four days ending Wed 5 Sept (Monument, LM) / 2-6 Sept, five days (RMG) / "nearly five" (Science Museum).
- Start time: 1am (Gazette, LFB, LM) / "shortly after midnight" (BL); Pepys was told about 3am.
- Cause: baker believed to have put the fire out, then a spark (Monument) / did not put it out (TNA) / unknowable (LM).
- Monument dates 1671-77 vs 1673-79; 202 vs 200 ft. Relief £16,400 vs £12,000. Plague deaths 68,595 vs 68,596.
