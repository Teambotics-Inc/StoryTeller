# Patagonia: research log

Brief. Subject: Patagonia, Inc. (the outdoor apparel company), not Yvon Chouinard the person nor the industry. Kind: company. Angle: a climbing-gear blacksmith's company keeps treating its own growth as the problem to solve, from hammered pitons to giving the company away, while critics keep testing the claim. Audience: general reader; independent study, user not affiliated. Boundaries: 1950s to Sept 2026; English-language public sources. Assumption: independent study, user name not supplied, so credit carries no personal name.

All sources accessed 2026-09-30. patagonia.com returned a "site maintenance" shell to the plain HTTP fetcher (WebFetch) but rendered fully in a real browser, so all patagonia.com pages below were read in a browser. "(summary)" = read only through a summarising fetcher, treat as lower confidence.

### hist: Our Company History
- https://www.patagonia.com/company-history/ · Patagonia · accessed 2026-09-30 · tier 1
- Chouinard began climbing in 1953 "as a 14-year-old member of the Southern California Falconry Club" (copied)
- "In 1957, Yvon went to a junkyard and bought a used coal-fired forge, a 138-pound anvil and some tongs and hammers" (copied); pitons forged two an hour, sold for $1.50 each
- "In 1965, Yvon went into partnership with Tom Frost and started Chouinard Equipment." "During the nine years that Frost and Chouinard were partners, they redesigned and improved almost every climbing tool" (copied)
- Design principle quoted from Saint-Exupery: "perfection is finally attained not when there is no longer anything to add, but when there is no longer anything to take away" (copied)
- By 1970 largest US climbing hardware supplier; "also become an environmental villain because its gear was damaging the rock"; decided to minimise the piton business
- "We introduced them in the first Chouinard Equipment catalog in 1972" (aluminium chocks); 14-page Doug Robinson essay "paving the way for future environmental essays in Patagonia catalogs"
- Note: the page text I captured ends at the chocks story; it says nothing about 1973 or the name.

### own: Yvon Chouinard Donates Patagonia to Fight Climate Crisis
- https://www.patagonia.com/ownership/ · Patagonia · tier 1 (company's own account)
- Letter dated September 14, 2022. "I never wanted to be a businessman. I started as a craftsman, making climbing gear for my friends and myself, then got into apparel." (copied)
- "More recently, in 2018, we changed the company's purpose to: We're in business to save our home planet." (copied)
- Options rejected: sell (new owner may not keep values) or go public ("What a disaster that would have been"). "Instead of "going public," you could say we're "going purpose."" (copied)
- FAQ: Holdfast Collective owns 98% and all nonvoting stock; Patagonia Purpose Trust owns 2% and all voting stock. Holdfast is a 501(c)(4) and "can advocate for causes and political candidates". Excess profits after reinvestment go to Holdfast as a dividend. Patagonia remains for-profit, certified B Corp, California benefit corporation. 1% of sales commitment now in charter, cannot change without Trust approval. Chouinard family guides the Trust; board includes Kris Tompkins, Dan Emmett, Dr. Ayana Elizabeth Johnson, Charles Conn (chair), Ryan Gellert (CEO).
- FAQ: "This is not an excuse to ignore the real tension we'll continue to face between growth and the environmental impact of our operations." (copied)
- Wording trap inside this page: the letter says "100% of the company's voting stock transfers to the Patagonia Purpose Trust" and 100% of nonvoting stock to Holdfast (100% of each class), the FAQ says Holdfast owns 98% of the company, Trust 2%. Consistent (classes vs share of total), but easy to misread.

### pfas: Made Without PFAS
- https://www.patagonia.com/our-footprint/pfas.html · tier 1
- "Between 2013 and 2016, we were able to fully phase out the use of long-chain (or C8) fluorocarbon-based treatments"; switched to C6; "new research emerged showing that C6 is just as detrimental" (copied)
- "In Fall 2019, we launched our first products with DWR finishes made without intentionally added PFAS"
- "For the Spring 2025 season and beyond, 100% of our new products are made without intentionally added PFAS." (copied). Page says both "more than 15 years of effort" and "a journey of nearly 15 years" (minor inconsistency)

### cotton: Cotton for Change
- https://www.patagonia.com/our-footprint/organic-cotton.html · tier 1
- "In 1996, we made a commitment to exclusively use 100% virgin cotton grown with organic practices" (copied)

### poly: Polyester

### onep: 1% for the Planet
- https://www.patagonia.com/one-percent-for-the-planet.html · tier 1
- "Since 1985, Patagonia has pledged 1% of sales to the preservation and restoration of the natural environment." "over $140 million in cash and in-kind donations" (copied)
- "In 2002, founder of Patagonia, Yvon Chouinard, and Craig Mathews, owner of Blue Ribbon Flies, created a non-profit corporation" for other businesses to do the same.
- Date conflict on when giving began: this page 1985; Action Works page "Since 1972"; Inc. 1992 "began tithing in 1984"; Wikipedia (summary) 1985 in one article and 1986 (10% of profits or 1% of sales, whichever greater) in the Chouinard article.

### dbtj: Don't Buy This Jacket, Black Friday and the New York Times
- https://www.patagonia.com/stories/dont-buy-this-jacket-black-friday-and-the-new-york-times/story-18615.html · tier 1 · first published Nov 25, 2011, updated with 2022 notes
- "We should be the only retailer in the country asking people to buy less on Black Friday." (copied)
- Each Patagonia garment "emits several times its weight in greenhouse gases, generates at least another half garment's worth of scrap, and draws down copious amounts of freshwater" (copied, company's own admission)
- Acknowledges the hypocrisy charge: "What do we tell customers who accuse us of hypocrisy?"
- Pledge: Reduce, Repair, Reuse, Recycle, Reimagine. Editor's notes: Common Threads Initiative evolved into Our Footprint; 2018 purpose change; Worn Wear now exists.
- Earlier purpose quoted: "to inspire and implement solutions to the environmental crisis"

### climate: Climate Goals

### b17: Coalition sues over Bears Ears (2017 press release)
- https://www.patagoniaworks.com/press/2017/12/11/broad-coalition-sues-to-stop-trump-administrations-unlawful-dismemberment-of-the-bears-ears-national-monument · Patagonia Works · tier 1 (party to the suit)
- Plaintiffs include Patagonia Works, Utah Dine Bikeyah, Friends of Cedar Mesa, Archaeology Southwest, Conservation Lands Foundation, Access Fund, Society for Vertebrate Paleontology, National Trust for Historic Preservation; filed in federal court in Washington, D.C.
- Claims the proclamation "amounts to the largest elimination of protected areas in American history"; revokes status for 85% of the monument area (plaintiffs' characterisation)

### b26: Patagonia Joins Environmental Groups in Suing Trump Over Bears Ears
- https://www.outsideonline.com/outdoor-adventure/environment/patagonia-lawsuit-bears-ears-trump/ · Outside · published September 3, 2026 · tier 2
- Suit filed September 2 in the US District Court for DC; challenges reduction "from 1.31 million acres to about 121,000 acres on July 13, 2026"; seeks to revive the 2017 filings, which courts "administratively closed" in 2021 when Biden reinstated boundaries. Quotes Patagonia deputy general counsel Rob Tadlock and CEO Ryan Gellert. Outcome pending.
- Headline says 91% cut; body says the monuments' combined reduction is "nearly 3 million acres" (Bears Ears plus Grand Staircase-Escalante).

### sierra: How Patagonia Surfed Around Death and Taxes
- https://www.sierraclub.org/sierra/how-patagonia-surfed-around-death-and-taxes · Sierra magazine, Jen Rose Smith · Sept 18, 2022 · tier 2
- Patagonia projects Holdfast will spend or donate $100 million each year.
- NYU tax professor Daniel Hemel: "instead of paying $1.2 billion in gift tax, they're paying $17 million in tax." He stresses this does not undercut the impact. A company adviser told the NYT it brings "no tax benefit"; Hemel says that is incorrect.

### grist: Two ways to think about Patagonia's $3 billion climate donation
- https://grist.org/accountability/patagonia-turns-over-company-fight-climate-change/ · Grist · Sept 20, 2022 · tier 2
- Giveaway valued at roughly $3 billion, no charitable deduction, family paid $17.5 million on the trust transfer (Sierra: $17 million; small discrepancy).
- Northeastern's Matthew Nisbet: "an escalating zero-sum political arms race". 501(c)(4)s need not disclose donors. Hemel called it "substantively similar" to Barre Seid's $1.6bn transfer.

### ecchr: Human rights violations off the rack
- https://www.ecchr.eu/en/press-release/human-rights-violations-off-the-rack/ · ECCHR · 2 Dec 2021 · tier 2 (complainant)
- Criminal complaint in the Netherlands against Patagonia, Nike, C&A, State of Art: they "may have been directly or indirectly complicit in the forced labor of members of the Uyghur population". An allegation, not a finding. Patagonia's response was not in this page.

### inc92: Lost in Patagonia (1992 profile)
- https://www.inc.com/magazine/19920801/4210.html · Inc., Edward O. Welles · Aug 1 1992 · tier 2
- "sales have soared from roughly $3 million in 1979 to $117 million in the latest fiscal year" (copied)
- "In July 1991 Patagonia, always known for "taking care of its own," laid off 120 people, or roughly 20% of its work force" (copied). Chouinard: "We were on a suicide course."
- Chouinard Equipment "declared Chapter 11 three years ago, when its liability premiums went through the roof" and was sold to former Patagonia employees.
- "Patagonia began tithing in 1984, distributing 10% of pretax profits (now 1% of sales)"
